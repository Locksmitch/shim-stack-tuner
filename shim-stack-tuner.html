▾Shim stack & damping force

Live stack preview & deflection animation — updates as you edit
?

Drawn straight from your shim table: each colored band is one row (clamp/nut rows in tan), in stack order, clamped at the ID on the left and bending along the computed deflection curve. Drag the slider to load the stack. A Float > 0 row sits above a real air gap (lighter, dashed edge until it engages) — the stack below bends up, closes the gap from the outer edge, and carries the floating shim from there. Shim #1 sits on the valve face; bigger/thicker shims near the face stiffen the stack most, since fluid pressure loads the outer edge near the port.

?

List shims from the valve face outward — group identical shims using the Count column. Each row has its own unit, so a metric shim and an imported inch shim can sit in the same stack. Set a row to Delta T to model a three-lobed shim with less effective bending stiffness than an equivalent round disc. If your stack has a separate clamp washer and/or nut, add them as regular rows too.

Count Diameter Thickness Float
?

Float is normally 0 (shim always bonded/engaged, the default). Give a "backup" or crossover shim a Float > 0 and it stays disengaged — contributing nothing — until the stack's local deflection right at that shim's own radius reaches the Float value, at which point it locks in and stiffens the stack from then on. That's what lets a stack be genuinely soft at low speed and stiffen at high speed (or vice versa), not just scaled up or down.

Reference diagram — what to measure
  • D.rod — shaft/rod diameter through the seal
  • D.valve — outer diameter of the valve/piston
  • Shims above the piston = compression; below = rebound. Simplified, not to scale.
Live diagram — valve port face (updates as you edit)
  • r.port — center to the port's inner edge
  • d.port — port's radial depth
  • w.port — port's width at the outer edge
  • shaft — D.rod bore
📷 Estimate r.port / d.port / w.port from a photo
?

Upload a straight-on photo of the valve face. Click 3 points anywhere along the valve's outer edge to set the scale from your D.valve value above — any 3 points on the rim will do, no need to find the center or opposite points. Then click all the way around one port's actual boundary — as many points as you like, rounded/kidney/D-shaped ports are fine — and press "Finish this port". Trace a couple more ports for a more reliable average, or just note how many more you can see. Each click snaps to the nearest sharp edge in the photo automatically. Works best on a reasonably straight-on shot; a steep angle will skew the numbers.
